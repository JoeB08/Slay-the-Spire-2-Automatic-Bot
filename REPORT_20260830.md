## 0b. Fixes from the post-set review

All from live observations, all verified, 448 tests green.

**Over-blocking was the real problem, not under-blocking.** Measured across the
set: **65 turns ended with 10+ Block spare against 3 that ended short.** Damage
taken per run rose to 120 from ~107, because energy spent on unneeded Block
makes fights last longer, and longer fights take more total damage. The two
complaints -- "underblocking a lot" and "blocked unnecessarily" -- are the same
defect seen from either end.

* **Leg Sweep was unplayable.** The rule writing off Weak cards when nothing is
  attacking exempted damage and poison but **not Block** -- so Leg Sweep
  ("Apply 2 Weak. Gain 12 Block") was filtered out of the hand entirely. The
  bot stacked two Defends for 10 instead of one Leg Sweep for 12, and on a free
  turn played Backflip into 18 Block against 0 incoming.
* **`_is_defensive_only`** now keeps Block-plus-Weak cards out of the *value*
  step when nothing is winding up, so a free turn goes to damage instead of
  Block -- while Leg Sweep is still chosen over plain Defends when Block is
  needed, and still beats a Defend on a free turn (the Weak carries).

**Discarding Block that was needed.** The "surplus block" check summed every
Block card in hand *regardless of cost*: an unaffordable Dash (10 Block, 2
energy, 1 energy left) read as "someone else covers it" and the affordable
Defend got pitched. Now only Block we can actually play counts as cover.

**Duplicate intake.** Nothing stopped a fourth Prepared ("Draw 1 card. Discard
1 card."). A flat penalty could not work -- Prepared scores 101 on pick rate
alone -- so copies now *decay* the score: 0.45x per extra copy for cards whose
only job is moving cards, 0.80x for cards that genuinely want copies. Result:
Prepared caps at 3, Blade Dance keeps being taken.

**Snakebite and Defend discards, checked and cleared.** Snakebite ranks
`(-1, -7, 0)` -- the trailing 0 is Retain, sorting it away from the discard --
and it sits in the attack tier because poison counts as damage. In a normal
hand it correctly pitches a Defend instead. The 11 live Snakebite discards were
hands where it was genuinely the least-bad pitch.

**Tooling trap found:** importing `bot.loop` opens a fresh decision log as a
side effect, so analysis scripts left 19 empty logs behind -- and
`_latest_log()` picked the newest by mtime, landing on an empty file and
silently reporting "nothing found". Both scripts now skip empty logs. This is
the fifth log-vs-reality trap on this project.

## 0c. Changes made AFTER the 10-run set started

The running set contains the Leg Sweep / over-blocking fix and the enchant-loop
fix, and **none of these**:

**`Asleep` — Lagavulin Matriarch.** Its text is "Awakens upon losing HP **or
after 3 turns**", so it wakes on its own regardless and any damage merely
spends the free turns early -- into 12 Plating that absorbs a Strike anyway.
The bot now banks those turns on Powers and setup, and only wakes it early for
a real payload (>= 30 reachable damage) or an outright kill.

**The act's boss is now known from floor 1.** The map payload carries
`"boss": {"id": "LAGAVULIN_MATRIARCH_BOSS", "name": "Lagavulin Matriarch"}` and
nothing had ever read it -- every card-reward decision was made blind to the
fight the whole act builds toward, which is where runs actually end. New module
`bot/strategy/boss_intel.py`. For Lagavulin it biases the build toward
**poison** (which ignores Block) and adds weight to **Block** cards (to survive
the 23s while poison ticks). Verified: a shiv-leaning deck commits to shiv under
Soul Fysh and to poison under Lagavulin.

**Upgrades favour attacks early.** Upgrading Prepared ("Draw 1 card. Discard 1
card.") buys a slightly better cycler; in act 1 the deck is mostly starters and
every fight is a damage race. Attacks get a bonus and pure cyclers a penalty
below floor 17. **Energy generation is explicitly not "pure utility"** --
Adrenaline is a genuine engine piece and still wins at any point.

**Sly cards are discarded, not paid for.** With Prepared+ (0 cost) and
Untouchable ("Sly. Gain 7 Block") in hand and 14 unblocked, the block step paid
energy for Untouchable -- when playing the free outlet and pitching it would
have given the same Block for nothing. Folded into the consolidated sequencing
rule: an outlet with a Sly card in hand loses its payoff if deferred.

**Unaffordable is not worthless.** Up My Sleeve ("Add 3 Shivs", 2 energy) is 12
damage, and its own text reduces its cost -- but at 1 energy it fell into the
"can't afford it" tier and was pitched ahead of two 6-damage Strikes, throwing
away a lethal. High-value unaffordable attacks now rank alongside affordable
ones on damage. *My first attempt put them one tier above, which still pitched
them first -- higher tier means more discardable.*

**Duplicate intake decay** and the **affordable-block-only** discard check also
landed before the set; see section 0b.

## 1. Lagavulin Matriarch has two mechanics the bot cannot see — grade A

It is the **single biggest killer of the current era: 6 of the last 40 runs**,
and 7 across all 89 recorded runs. Its kit, straight from 672 logged decisions:

    222 HP, attacks for 23, Strength +4
    Plating   "At the end of your turn, gain 12 Block."
    Asleep    "Awakens upon losing HP or after 3 turns."
    gives us  Greed ("Unplayable. Eternal."), Injury ("Unplayable.")

**Plating was modelled only for the player.** `_pending_block_from_status`
reads `gs.player`, so the *enemy* regaining 12 Block every turn was invisible.
A deck chipping for less than 12 a turn makes literally no progress while
taking 23 a turn back, and the bot could not see the wall — it committed to a
race it could never finish.

*Fixed:* `_enemy_block_regen` now reads it, and `_should_race` subtracts it
from our per-turn output. Poison is exempted, because poison bypasses Block
and is the actual answer to this fight.

**`Asleep` appears zero times in the codebase.** The bot does not know that a
sleeping enemy is harmless, nor that attacking wakes it. The correct line is
to spend those free turns on setup — poison, Powers, Block — and this is
almost certainly worth more than the Plating fix. **Not implemented**: the
right behaviour needs a judgement call I would rather you made. The options
are roughly "never attack while it sleeps, bank everything" versus "burst it
down before it wakes", and which is right depends on the deck.

## 1b. BLOCKING BUG — the enchant screen relaunched the game — grade A

**The most serious thing found today. You had to rescue the bot by hand.**

Gnarled Hammer ("Upon pickup, Enchant up to 3 Attacks with Sharp 3") opens
`Choose 3 cards to Enchant.` The bot selected three cards and confirmed --
**32 times**, every call returning `ok`, the screen never advancing:

    select_card 0   -> ok
    select_card 1   -> ok
    select_card 2   -> ok
    confirm_selection -> ok      ... same screen again

That produced **14 `stuck_detected` events**, and the freeze recovery
**killed and relaunched Slay the Spire 2 each time** -- which is what you saw.
The single-card version of the same screen works fine; only the 3-pick form
fails.

Two separate defects:

**1. The recovery was too destructive.** `_recover_from_stuck` assumed any
unchanging state is an engine freeze, on the reasoning that no API action can
unstick one. But the game here was perfectly healthy and answering `ok` --
only the *screen* was unsatisfiable. Relaunching threw the run away for
nothing. *Fixed:* the recovery now tries to leave the screen first
(`cancel_selection`, then `proceed`) on any selection-type screen, and only
relaunches if that fails. Worst case it skips one reward; before, it lost the
whole run.

**2. Why the confirm does not take is still unknown.** The API accepts every
call. `selected` is never populated in the payload, so the bot cannot verify
what the game thinks is selected -- the same trap as `can_confirm`, which is
`True` here from before anything is picked. I did not guess at a fix: it needs
one live experiment on that screen (try selecting only 2, or re-selecting the
same index, and watch `can_confirm`). **Until then the escape hatch is what
protects the run.**

### Related: enchanted Strikes were being removed as junk

`removal_priority` keyed on the card *name*, so a Strike reading "Deal 9
damage" scored 503 -- prime junk -- exactly like a fresh one. The bot would
enchant three Strikes with Sharp 3 and then delete them at the next shop.
Removal screens do expose the real text, so the fix reads it:

    Strike "Deal 6 damage."    -> 503   (still junk)
    Strike "Deal 9 damage."    ->  37   (Sharp 3: keep it)
    Strike "Deal 18 damage."   ->  28
    Defend "Gain 11 Block."    ->  34

## 2. Act 2 bosses are survival puzzles, not damage races — grade A

Both act-2 bosses kill through a mechanic rather than a health bar, and both
were being played as ordinary fights:

**The Insatiable** (321 HP) applies `Sandpit` — *"In 4 turns, you will be
eaten and die."* The HP total is bait. The answer is `Frantic Escape`, a
Status card whose cost climbs by 1 each play. *Fixed* (death clock), including

**Knowledge Demon** (379 HP, and it heals) forces a choice between two

so the ranking tied and `max()` took whatever the game listed first — which
was recurring self-damage, twice. That run died at floor 33 taking 13 damage a
turn from its own choices. *Fixed*, with the ordering you specified.

## 4. Smaller defects found and fixed today

Each came from a specific observed play, and each is verified by replaying the
real payload:

* **Grand Finale+ was invisible.** The check was an exact string match on
  `"Grand Finale"`, so an upgraded copy slipped past every guard — a live run
  held it in a 20-card deck and kept taking card rewards. Detection now uses
  `base_name`, and holding it makes every other card removable at shops, rest
  sites and events.
* **Potions burned on trivial turns.** `Petrified Toad` grants guaranteed
  drops, so with a full belt `would_overflow` was *permanently* true and every
  turn became a candidate for burning a potion to make room. It drank Liquid
  Memories at 21 HP against an 8-damage trash monster. The pressure never
  expires, so waiting for a turn where the potion does something costs nothing.
* **Swift Potion into a full hand.** `_draw_would_be_wasted` existed but was
  wired into exactly one branch, so every other path could still drink it.
* **Blade Dance at the hand cap** — it Exhausts, so playing it with no room
  spent the card permanently for nothing.
* **Slimed was discarded rather than exhausted.** Discarding recycles it into
  the deck; playing it removes it for good. The discard should fall on dead
  weight we have no other way to remove.
* **Treasure chests could be walked past.** `decide_treasure` proceeded the
  instant the relic list read empty, which also happens when the payload has
  not populated. A floor-26 chest was missed outright — the relic count went
  in at 8 and came out at 8. It now attempts the claim up to three times.
* **Orientation.** `Surrounded` / `Back Attack` were unmodelled, so a Crusher
  telegraphing 20 was read as 20 when it lands for 30. Attacking an enemy
  turns you to face it, so targeting now prefers an attacker behind us.

## 6. Measurement notes — read before trusting any number

* **The logged `incoming` field is not what the bot reasoned with.** A row
  showed `incoming: 23` while `decide()` correctly computed **0**, because the
  enemy was already dead to poison and never gets a turn. `loop.py` writes an
  unfiltered summary. Three false findings have come from log-vs-payload
  differences before; this is a fourth trap.
* **Per-card success data is badly confounded.** Deck is recorded at *death*,
  so cards that look strong are mostly cards that deep runs had time to
  acquire. Infection scores −5.7 floors and is not even a choice. To get real
  numbers the deck needs recording at a *fixed floor*, which cannot be
  reconstructed from existing logs.
* **New tool:** `python scripts/audit_decisions.py` scans a decision log for
  the defect classes we keep finding — wasted energy, zero-value plays, missed
  lethals, draws into a full hand, pointless potions, overkill. It found 11
  candidates in 1791 decisions, of which **zero survived replay** — the good
  outcome. Its own first version had a precedence bug that flagged all 630
  plays; the fix is in.
